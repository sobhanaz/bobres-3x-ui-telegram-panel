# 04 - Dashboard pages and roles

Stack: Vue 3 + Vite + TypeScript (embedded in `core` via embed.FS), JSON API on `core`, behind the gateway. See PLAN.md §12. Branded per install (logo, colors, name).
Sessions: secure cookie, CSRF. Login with a one-time link from the bot (or `bobres admin link`), or with a password that always needs a TOTP code. The owner can also sign in with no Telegram at all: the installer (or `bobres admin password`) issues a random initial password for the login `owner`; it opens a restricted session where only My account works (a forced screen to choose a new password and turn on 2FA, with no menu), and the rest of the dashboard follows once the first code is confirmed (see 06-installer.md, Owner login without Telegram).
Decisions, milestones and status: `superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-phase4-dashboard-design.md`. All 17 pages below are
built (Phase 4, 2026-10-12), and page 18, Reports, was added after v0.1.1; the few parts that are not built are marked.

## Roles and permissions
| Role | Scope |
|---|---|
| owner | Everything incl. license, staff, gateway secrets, danger zone |
| admin | Users, orders, plans, discounts, broadcasts, settings, branding, 3x-ui servers (write-only tokens), reports, audit log (no staff, license, gateway secrets or payment details) |
| support | Tickets, read-only users/orders, limited actions (extend a service, read it from the panel) |
| reseller | Not a staff role and no dashboard access: works only in the Telegram bot and the Mini App (the reseller panel and section: buy for customers, pause, resume, delete and keep notes on their own customers' services, see the customers who joined through their link). Owner and admin manage resellers on the Resellers page (`resellers.write`) |
| finance (optional) | Payments, ledger, reports, exports, no user edits. Not built: owner, admin and support cover v1 (`reports.read` is its own flag, so a finance role can be given it later) |
Permissions are a list of granular flags (e.g. `users.write`, `wallet.adjust`, `reports.read`) so custom roles are possible later. Roles are defined in code (`internal/core/web/perms.go`), so a new flag reaches existing installs with the update and needs no migration: `reports.read` is held by owner and admin, not by support.
Every write goes to `audit_log`; sensitive actions (balance adjust, refund, ban) need a reason field. A bulk purchase is one `order.bulk` entry (the plan, the quantity, the total and the buyer, source `bot`) beside the ordinary order and wallet entries of each service. What a reseller does to their own services is in it too: pausing, resuming and deleting a service are `subscription.disable`, `subscription.enable` and `subscription.delete` with the reseller as the actor (role reseller), source `bot` (the Mini App is the bot's second face) and the reason "paused by the reseller", "resumed by the reseller" or "deleted by the reseller". A service the reseller paused can be told from one a staff member paused by the actor; the reseller cannot resume the second.

## Pages
1. **Overview:** revenue (only in the answer for staff with `reports.read`: support does not get it), new users, active services, expiring soon, failed provisions, gateway health (on, off, details missing, failing), panel health per server (switched-off servers are not counted).
2. **Users:** search/filter, profile (services, orders, wallet, tickets), ban, adjust balance, impersonate-view (read-only; not built).
3. **Services:** all subscriptions, status, usage, extend, reset traffic, delete, resync from 3x-ui, and the address of the service's branded page with a Regenerate button (`services.write`, with a reason; [15-subscription-page.md](15-subscription-page.md)).
4. **Plans:** CRUD, trial flag, ordering, enable/disable, the servers a plan's new services may use (empty = any) and, per server, the inbounds they join (empty = every attachable inbound; a choice wins over the Servers page's attach switch, only inbounds that are on in the panel are used, and a warning shows when a chosen inbound is gone from the panel) (reseller tier prices are set on the Resellers page).
5. **Orders and payments:** list, states, retry provisioning, refund, manual receipt review queue.
6. **Wallet and ledger:** read-only ledger explorer, exports (CSV).
7. **Discounts and referrals:** codes, limits, usage, referral rules.
   **Gift codes** (its own page next to Discounts, `discounts.write` like them; a code that adds a fixed amount of wallet
   credit when a customer applies it in the bot, unlike a discount, which takes money off a purchase): make one code of
   your own words (at least 8 characters, at least 5 different) or a batch of up to 500 generated ones (12 random
   characters from an alphabet without 0, O, 1, I and L, shown as `ABCD-EFGH-JKMN`, about 59 bits, with an optional prefix of
   at most 8 letters or digits), each with an amount and currency, uses in all, uses per customer, an optional expiry and a
   note. **The codes are shown once**, in a dialog that copies them or downloads them as a CSV (UTF-8 with a byte-order
   mark, formulas quoted); core keeps only a keyed hash and the last four characters, so they cannot be shown again. The
   list shows the last four characters, the value, used / in all, status (active, used up, expired, switched off), the
   expiry, the last use and the note, filters by status and searches a note or the last four characters, exports as a CSV (the rules of [14-reports.md](14-reports.md#csv-files): English headers, UTC and local times, a Jalali column in a Jalali file),
   and has a switch per code (what a code paid stays paid) and a list of who redeemed it and when (also a CSV). A banner
   says when customers cannot use gift codes (the setting `gift_codes.enabled`, with a button to turn it on for staff who
   may) or when core has no master key and cannot make them.
8. **Resellers:** list (search, tier, in debt), tiers with a discount or fixed price per plan and a commission, make a customer a reseller (also from their page), credit limits, recording payments received, their services, customers, statement and orders.
9. **Servers (3x-ui):** several panels: URL, write-only token, subscription URL, weight, on/off for new services, version, health, services and plans per server, a connection test, and an inbound list whose attach toggles affect new services only. A panel on a private network (plain http allowed there) is the owner's to add, or to give another address or token; admins may rename, reweight or switch one off.
10. **Payment gateways (owner):** Zarinpal on/off, sandbox, write-only merchant id and proxy, test connection, payments of the last 7 and 30 days and recent check errors; automatic USDT (Phase 6a: on/off, the TRON address, an optional write-only TronGrid key and proxy, test connection, its report, and the unmatched deposits staff mark handled once they credited the customer by hand); the manual methods and Stars with their on/off switch and details (moved from Settings).
11. **Support:** ticket inbox, canned replies, assignment. The inbox filters by category: Payment, Service, Other and **Reseller application**, the category of a ticket the bot files when a customer taps Become a reseller and writes about themselves (the ticket page and the Mini App name it too; making the applicant a reseller is the Resellers page's action, as before).
12. **Broadcasts:** compose (fa/en), audience filter, schedule, progress.
13. **Branding and texts:** name, logo, colors, welcome message, all bot strings per language, legal links, currency.
    - **Help content** (a tab of Branding, `?tab=help`, `branding.write` like the texts): the FAQ and the tutorials the bot shows ([03-bot-ux.md](03-bot-ux.md)). Each list adds, edits, reorders (up and down), switches on or off and deletes entries; an entry has a title (the question) and a text in Persian and English, tutorials also up to 8 https URL buttons with a label per language. The editor checks as you type (lengths, a language with only half a pair, button addresses; core checks the markup and has the last word), and a live preview shows the entry as the bot shows it in either language with the fallback, how the FAQ will be laid out (one message or a list) and the support button. An edit made from an out-of-date copy is refused ("changed meanwhile"). Every change is audited as `help.*` (entity `help_entry`) and reaches the bot within seconds. An empty list offers the default content. API: `GET/POST /api/v1/help`, `POST /api/v1/help/check|order|defaults`, `PUT/DELETE /api/v1/help/{id}`, `PUT /api/v1/help/{id}/enabled`.
14. **Settings:** general, notifications, the daily sales summary in Telegram (`reports.daily_summary` on or off, `reports.daily_summary_time`), the Growth card (`growth.leaderboard.enabled`: the bot's Top users board of the customers who invited the most people, on by default, shown while the referral program is on), the Plans and bulk purchase card (`bot.plan_layout`: the bot's plan list as a table, a header row and each plan as a row of two buttons for its name and its price, or as a list with one button per plan; `bulk.enabled`: the Bulk purchase button on the bot's home menu and core's side of it, on by default; `bulk.max_quantity`: the most services one bulk purchase holds, 2 to 100, 20 by default), the **Required channels and groups** card (below), rate limits, maintenance mode, timezone.
    - **Required channels and groups** (`GET` and `PUT /api/v1/join-chats`; the list customers must join before they can use the bot or the Mini App, [03-bot-ux.md](03-bot-ux.md) "Required chats"). It replaces the old Join card (the generic `GET`/`PUT /api/v1/settings` still list and take `join.channel`, `join.link` and the new `join.label`, so nothing that used them breaks). One editable **list**: each row has the channel or group (a public one's `@name`, a `t.me` link or Persian digits are understood; a private one's `-100…` id), its join link (`https://t.me/…`, required for a private chat) and an optional name for the button (at most 32 characters), a **Check channel** button that asks the bot whether it can see the chat and is its administrator (a channel or a group; `POST /api/v1/settings/channel-check`), and, besides the first row, move up, move down and remove. The **first row is the setting `join.channel`** (with `join.link` and `join.label`) and stays first: the others are the extra chats and can be reordered among themselves; removing the first row makes the next one the first, and an empty list turns the requirement off. The card counts the rows against the license (**"2 of 1"** when more are saved than the plan counts), disables **Add** at the limit with a note that Pro has no limit and a link to the License tab, marks the rows the plan does not count **"Inactive: needs Pro"** (dashed, they stay saved and can be edited, reordered and removed), and checks every row where it is typed with the rules core applies (a chat once in the list, a private chat needs its link, `https://t.me/` links, names on one line). It is saved **all at once** (one `PUT` with `{"chats":[{"chat","link","label"}]}`): all or nothing, a refused row is named by its field (`chats.2.link`), and a chat added past the license is a `409` `{"error":"state","license":"max_join_chats"}` with the usual license sentence. **Owner only** to change (`join.write`, an owner-only flag like `license.write`); an admin sees the list read only (`GET` needs `settings.write`) and keeps `/set join.channel`, `join.link` and `join.label` in the bot, as before. The list holds at most 50 rows. Every change is in the audit log (`setting.set` for the first row; `join_chat.add`, `.update`, `.move`, `.remove` for the others, entity `join_chat`, the chat as its id), the extras are in every backup (the `core.join_chats` table, migration 00029), and the bot and the Mini App pick the new list up at once (core tells the bot to reload; the Mini App forgets its cached list).
15. **Staff:** invite, roles, 2FA status, sessions.
16. **System (owner and admin):** tabs. *Health*: every service (core, bot, payments, provisioner, backup) with its version, health and readiness checks, a warning when versions differ, and the 3x-ui servers and payment methods. *License*: what applies now (the free tier, the 30-day trial with its days left, or the license's tier) with whom to contact to buy or renew a license (the owner's `BOBRES_SELLER_CONTACT`, or "ask the seller you got BOBRES from" when none is set); the installed token's state (none, invalid with the reason, active, grace, expired), tier, ids, dates and days left, the domain and install binding, this install's id to copy, and each entitlement in effect with its use (a warning from 90%); the owner pastes or opens a token, checks it, and saves it with a reason, or activates one with a license key from the seller (05-license.md). *Updates*: the running and newest versions, with a **Check again** button (the bot looks at GitHub now and the page shows the result, including why a look failed), the update channel, and **Update now**. The channel is the owner's choice (stable, or beta which includes pre-releases; saved as the `updates.channel` setting, audited as `setting.set`; unset, the server's `BOBRES_UPDATE_CHANNEL` decides) and changing it makes the bot look again at once. **Update now** (the owner; `system.write`) opens a confirmation that names the versions and channel, says the dashboard and the bot are away for a few minutes while customers' VPN links keep working, and that a backup comes first and the update goes back on its own if the new version does not come up healthy; a pre-release needs its own tick, and an owner with a password login and an authenticator app gives a current code (as for replacing the password). Core never touches Docker: it records the request (audit `system.update.requested`, a Telegram notice to every owner) and writes `request.json` into a folder it shares with the host's updater (a systemd path unit, `bobres-updater.path`, installed by `bobres install` and `bobres update`), which runs the same signed, backed-up, rolling-back `bobres update` as the command line. The page then shows the steps (verify, backup, pull, restart, health check), the updater's sentence and, for the owner, the newest log lines; it says so and keeps trying while core restarts, and offers to reload the dashboard when the new version is up. The outcome (done, failed, rolled back, or never picked up) is audited and sent to every owner. Where the updater is not installed yet (an install from before it, or the folder not mounted) the page says updating from the dashboard is not available yet and keeps the `sudo bobres update` command to copy. An informational note shows when the newest release is newer than the license's update date (the update is offered all the same: security updates are never withheld). *Backups*: each backup with its size, Telegram delivery and off-site copy, a button to run one now, and the off-site storage's state. *HTTPS*: how each address the store is served on (the domain, the server's IP address, the Zarinpal domain) gets its certificate, with the issuer, expiry, whether browsers trust it, the live error and the HTTPS applier's last result (and a red line, "HTTPS gateway (Caddy) is not reachable", when Caddy answers on none of them); the owner switches between the domain's automatic certificate, a Let's Encrypt certificate for the server's IP address and an own certificate and key, with the warnings of each mode (`system.write`, audited as `system.tls.set`; the runbook is runbooks/https.md). *Logs* (owner only): the newest warnings and errors of every service, redacted. The Health tab also lists the open system problems. Restoring a backup is CLI only (`bobres restore`). Without a valid license the install runs on the free tier after its trial, and its limits refuse only new servers, services, staff, resellers and payment methods (a refusal names the limit and says to buy or renew a license); staff who see this page get a small chip in the top bar for the free tier, the trial (with its days left) or a license that needs a look. "Powered by BOBRES" shows at the foot of the dashboard and the login page on every install except one whose license includes white label.
17. **Audit log:** filterable, exportable.
18. **Reports** (`reports.read`: owner and admin; support has neither the page nor the revenue cards of the Overview, while single orders' amounts stay visible to support through Orders by design): revenue per day, week or month for a range (today, last 7 or 30 days, this month, last month, or two dates), always per currency (never summed), with paid orders (new, renewals, traffic packages), new customers, free trials, churn (services and customers lost after a 7-day grace period, and the services still inside it; trials, unlimited services and pending renewals are not counted), wallet top-ups, refunds, payments rejected by staff, failed or expired gateway payments and cancelled orders; a revenue chart per currency, the top plans and how orders were paid (wallet, staff-approved, gateway, each split by the provider that took the payment: Zarinpal, Stars, automatic USDT, card to card...); CSV files of the summary and of the paid orders, audited as `export.reports` with their outcome. A Calendar switch picks Gregorian (weeks from Monday, calendar months) or Jalali (weeks from Saturday, Jalali months named by month and year), and every date of the page follows it, in both directions: the days covered, the charts, the table, the custom range (dates typed or picked from a month grid in the calendar shown, converted exactly to the Gregorian days the API takes) and the CSV files' extra Jalali date column and names. The choice is kept in the browser; until one is made it is Jalali in Persian and Gregorian in English. All in the store's time zone (the Settings page, else `BOBRES_TIMEZONE`, else Asia/Tehran). A line under the hint says whether the daily summary in Telegram is on, at what time and in which zone, and links to its settings for staff who may change settings. Every number is defined in [14-reports.md](14-reports.md).
