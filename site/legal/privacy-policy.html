Version: draft 0.1, [OWNER: date]. It covers two things, and says for each who is responsible:

  1. The BOBRES software on an Operator's server: what it stores about the Operator's customers and staff.
  2. The Vendor's license service and website: what the Vendor receives from an Operator.

"The Vendor" is [OWNER: legal name]. "The Operator" is whoever runs a BOBRES install. "Customers" are the people who buy from the Operator's store.

1. Who is responsible for what

  • The Operator is the controller of the customers' data. BOBRES runs on the Operator's own server, with the Operator's database, bot, domain and payment accounts. The Operator decides why and how the data is used, and answers customers' requests.
  • The Vendor never receives the customers' data. It is not stored on, copied to or sent to any Vendor system. The Vendor is therefore neither the controller nor the processor of it. [LAWYER: confirm these roles, and whether any data-processing clause is needed anyway.]
  • The Vendor is the controller of what the license service receives (section 4), and of the Operator's contact and payment records.

BOBRES does not ask for a phone number, an e-mail address or a name. It does not collect browsing or traffic content: it reads only how much traffic a service used from the 3x-ui panel.

3. Where it goes

  • The Operator's server: PostgreSQL and Redis in Docker volumes. Redis holds only what can be rebuilt.
  • The 3x-ui panels the Operator adds: they hold the VPN client records (each service's client name and its usage).
  • Telegram. Customers talk to the bot through Telegram, so Telegram handles those messages under its own policy. The bot's token is held by the Operator.
  • Payment providers the Operator switches on (Zarinpal, Telegram Stars, the TRON network for automatic USDT) receive what a payment needs, under their own policies. BOBRES asks for no card numbers, but a receipt image that a customer sends may show some.
  • Backups. Each day the install makes an encrypted backup. It holds all the data above and the store's keys. The newest 7 stay on the server, each is sent to the Operator on Telegram, and the Operator may add copies in S3-compatible storage (the newest 30 by default). The files are encrypted with the backup key, which stays on the Operator's server and is shown to the Operator. The Operator keeps the key safe: whoever has a backup and the key can read everything.

4. What the Vendor receives

Only an install with BOBRES_LICENSE_SERVER set talks to the Vendor. BOBRES has no telemetry.

The license server stores:

  • the license: a hash of the key (never the key), the customer name the Vendor gave it, the tier, the dates and the entitlements;
  • each activation: the domain, the install id, the version and when it was last seen;
  • an audit of what the Vendor did and of refused requests, with the IP address of requests from the network.

Logs hold license ids, domains, install ids, IP addresses and refusal codes. They never hold a key or a token.

  • Customers ask the Operator. The Operator can delete a customer's VPN service, ban a customer and answer from the data in the dashboard. BOBRES has no one-click export or erasure of a customer's data yet; because the ledger and the audit log are append-only, erasing a customer means anonymizing, not deleting, what the ledger holds. 7. Security

Each service has its own database role and token; secrets are stored encrypted with a key per service; staff log in with a password and an authenticator code; the dashboard and the license service use HTTPS; the license service keeps only key hashes. No system is perfectly secure. The Operator reports a problem to [OWNER: security contact]. The server's security is the Operator's job: the Vendor cannot reach it.
